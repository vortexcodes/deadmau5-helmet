# Making one song's show for the deadmau5 helmet

You are choreographing one song for a real wearable LED helmet (deadmau5-style mau5head), for tonight's Halloween party. The owner's words: "Create these really good animations ... especially ones like the Tiki Tiki one, which was really good because it was unique. Keep doing these cool, unique ones for each song." Read `shows/scenes/TIKI TIKI - Slowed.scene.json` and its generator `tools/showkit/songs/tiki/write_treatment.py` first: that is the bar. A show is a story with its own world, palette and characters; motifs come back when the music does; builds visibly grow; drops land exactly on the bass; breakdowns strip back to almost nothing; the ears play as an instrument of their own. It must not look like a sequence of effects, and it must not look like any other show.

Working directory: the repo root (run everything from there). `KIT=tools/showkit`. Your scratch files go in `$KIT/songs/<short song name>/`.

## Hard rules
- Only write these files for your song: `shows/scenes/<base>.beats`, `shows/scenes/<base>.scene.json`, `shows/scenes/<base>.sprites.json`, and what the tools make: `shows/<base>.json`, `shows/<base>.analysis.npz`. Nine other authors are working beside you on other songs. Never edit any code, any test, or another song's file. If you truly need an engine feature, finish without it and say so in your report.
- NEVER contact the helmet (192.168.10.172, 4.3.2.1, or anything scanning the network). Do not run `mau5show.py play`, `mau5offline.py`, `mau5ctl`, `test_mau5.py`, or anything that starts a browser. Do not commit. Do not delete MP3s.
- Text on the helmet: the song's title words only. Never spell out any other lyric.
- No power limit gets changed, ever. If a scene is too hot, make the scene lighter.
- Time: check `date`. Stop polishing and return by the deadline in your task. A complete, correct show beats a perfect half of one, so get a whole-song draft compiling early, then improve it.

## The steps
1. `python3 $KIT/grid.py scan "shows/<base>.mp3"` then `fold` over the song. Measure the grid: tempo flat across the song within about 10 ms per 8 bars; a seam or tempo change shows as a step. Decide the bar line from the music, not from the estimator: phrases turn on it and the drop's first kick is step 0 of a bar. The estimator puts bars half a bar off regularly, and a bar line wrong by a beat or two ruins every drop. Use `grid.py scan`'s phrase positions, `fold`, and the spectrogram to be sure. Then `grid.py write "<base>" --spb S --anchor T` (it builds the show), and `grid.py check "<base>"`: worst under 15 ms where the music is clear. A song that truly changes tempo needs holds or, if it speeds up, tell me; do not ship a wrong grid.
2. Understand the song. `python3 $KIT/songmap.py "<base>"` for the bar map with chord fingerprints, `python3 $KIT/spectro.py "<base>" out.png [from to]` and read the PNG to see builds, drops and breakdowns, and `python3 mau5scene.py bars "<base>"`. Write down its form in bars. Zoom into each transition: a mark must be on the bar the music changes, checked, not guessed.
3. Draw your world. Sprites go in `shows/scenes/<base>.sprites.json` as `{"<song>_<thing>": ["rows", ...]}`, every row the same length, `.` is off and anything else is lit; prefix every name with the song. Try them with `python3 $KIT/spritetry.py "<base>" out.png name ...` and read the PNG. Redraw until it reads. The shell is not a screen: it has NO LEDs across the middle of the face (y about 50 to -90 mm). It is a dome over the forehead and eyes (y 58 to 170) and a side and jaw arc down to y -102. So design for that, as the Tiki idol does (brow and eyes up top, teeth along the jaw). About 17 x 17 cells over the whole shell with `y_lo:-108, y_hi:166, x_half:171` is the most it resolves, and shapes built from solid blobs read far better than outlines.
4. Write the treatment with a small Python generator in your scratch folder (see Tiki's), using `from fmt import save` with `sys.path.insert(0, KIT)`, then `python3 mau5scene.py compile "<base>"` and fix what it refuses. Put a real `about` string in it describing the concept, the same style as Tiki's.
5. Look at it. `python3 $KIT/sheet.py "<base>" out.png 40:drop 40.25 41 ...` renders tiles as the helmet will really look after the power guard, and prints how bright shell and ears end up. READ THE PNG. Does it read as your idea? Is the shell visible next to the ears? Check every major section, each hit, and both a kick and the gap after it. Iterate.
6. `python3 mau5scene.py check "<base>"`: every scene through the power guard. Heat under about 40%, and no scene "at the sustained floor". `python3 $KIT/grid.py check "<base>"` again at the end.

## The treatment format
Bars, not seconds: bar b is `t0 + 4 b spb` on your grid. Top level: `v:1, show, about, lead:0, marks{name:bar or "other+2"}, palettes{name:[[r,g,b],...] or {"stops":[...],"wrap":true}}, motifs{name: layer or [layers]}, scenes[], hits[]`. A value may be a number, `[from,to]` (a ramp over the scene), `{"<bar or mark>":v,...}` (steps at bars), or `{"band":"bass|mid|high","lo":a,"hi":b,"punch":true|false}` (driven by the track, resolved live; the phone has these curves). Scenes tile the song, from bar -1 (a gap is dark): `{"name","from","to","pal","pump":{"pat":"x...","step":0.25,"depth":0.2,"decay":0.5},"out":beats of darkness before the next scene (max 4),"layers":[...]}`; `like:"earlier scene"` copies one and `patch:{"layerIndex":{...}}`, `add:[...]` or `each:{...}` change it (patch indexes the authored layers before motif expansion). Hits are moments over a scene: `{"name","at":bar,"beats":n,"pal","layers":[...]}`. Limits: 6 layers a scene, 3 a hit, 2 hits at once, one text at a time.

Every layer: `fx, on (region), mix ("add" default, "max", "over": over paints what is brighter than 4 and leaves the rest), pal, gain, c (palette position 0-1), gate {"pat":"x...o...","step":0.25 beats,"decay":beats,"floor":0..1} (rhythm as a gain), env {"in":beats,"out":beats}`. Regions, joinable with +: `all shell ears ear1 ear2 tubes rims tube0 tube1 tube2 tubes1 tubes2 rim1 rim2 left right face jaw`. Each ear is a rim (a circle) and three tubes numbered 0 (lowest) to 2.

Effects:
- `fill`: one colour on a region.
- `grad`: a moving palette gradient along `axis`, warped by `axis2`. Axes: `x` left-right 0-1, `y` up the shell 0-1, `r` radius from each part's own centre 0-1, `a` angle round each part's own centre 0-1 (each ear and the shell separately, so radar sweeps and spirals work), `al` along an ear tube 0-1, `u` along the chain. `scale, speed (cycles a beat), ofs, warp, wscale, wspeed, lo, hi, wave ("tri" or "saw")`. A palette running through black makes stripes: scale 3 to 6 on x makes bars and cages, on y bands and scanlines.
- `sweep`: a band (`mode:"band"`, `width`) or a filled front (`mode:"fill"`, `soft`) moving along `axis` from `from` to `to`: `shape:"p"` once over the scene, `"saw"` or `"tri"` repeating at `speed` a beat. `from` and `to` may be band values, which makes a VU meter on `al`. `cgrad` moves colour along the axis.
- `sparks`: `rate` per beat (a number or keys), `life` beats, `spread` colour spread, `nbr` neighbours' share, `loop` repeat length so the pattern recurs, `seed`. life x rate at most 8.
- `blades`: the ear tubes firing upward: `seq:["0","1","2","012"]` tube sets per step, `step` beats (no faster than 0.25), `rise`, `keep`, `ctip`, `rim`, `side` ("both","alt","ear1","ear2"). This is the ears' own instrument.
- `orb` (new): a soft disc on the flat front view, across shell and ears: `x, y` in mm (x -305 to 325 across both ears, y -102 to 288), `rad, soft` mm, `wx, wy` swing amount mm, `xf, yf` swings a beat, `xp, yp` phase, `cgrad`. Suns, moons, spotlights, pupils, a ball going ear to ear.
- `twinkle` (new): each LED on its own sine: `rate` times a beat (number or keys), `sharp` power (high = brief glints), `dens` share of LEDs taking part, `spread`, `seed`. Starfields, film grain, radio static, leopard spots.
- `preset`: `name:"sprite"` with `params:{sprite:"name" or "a|b|c" (flip book), rate:frames a second, color:"palette@pos" or [r,g,b], y_lo,y_hi,x_half, dx,dy mm, vx,vy mm a second, wx,wy,wf ellipse swing mm and cycles a second, blink}`, time starting from the scene's start, shell only; `name:"text"` with `fit:true` crossing the face once over the scene or hit, `params:{text,color}`; `name:"eyes"` with `params:{color,period,size,y,spread,blink_every}`, a pair of soft eyes looking side to side.

## What the hardware can do
621 LEDs: shell 397, each ear 112 (three tubes and a rim). LEDs sit behind diffusers, so soft shapes and big motion read best; fine detail does not. On the shell: gates and pumps no faster than 8th notes (the compiler refuses faster, because whole-shell flicker reads as a fault). Ears can go faster. Current is tight: the sustained budget is about 300 mA of LED current across the whole helmet with short bursts to 1 A, and when the ears are loud the guard takes it from the shell, so keep shell gains near Tiki's (0.14 to 0.3 for full-shell layers, more for small lit areas) and ear layers 0.25 to 0.7, and confirm in `sheet.py` that the shell is really visible. Bright full-helmet white is for one moment in the whole song at most. Dark is an effect: contrast is most of what makes a hit land.

Song-specific concept follows in your task. It is a starting point. If the music says otherwise, follow the music, and keep it unique.

## Added after the first run: the long set
These shows also play back to back in a set of up to 90 minutes. The helmet can shed 11 W for ever (7.4 W of that is the LEDs' own idle draw), and the 3000 J heat bucket only covers what goes above that. When songs run hotter, heat builds up song after song, and after about 20 minutes every hit in the rest of the set gets flattened by the guard. So besides the checks above, run `python3 $KIT/power.py "<base>"` and aim for a whole-song mean of 11.5 W or less, and never above 12.5 W. Heat at the end of the song should be under about 15%. Get there with contrast rather than by dimming everything: darker verses and breakdowns, fewer full-shell layers, more small lit shapes, ears dark between hits. Peaks can stay as high as they are. A dark helmet with one bright thing moving is both cheaper and better-looking than a washed-out one. Do not change any limit.


## Correction: previews before about 13:06 were flattering
`$KIT/sheet.py` used to skip the power guard. It now goes through the real guard, so anything judged by the old sheet must be judged again. What the guard does that matters: the shell region is capped near 300 mA (mean about 10 of 255) with a soft knee, so anything above about 64 comes out near 62 and hues shift; when the whole frame hits 1000 mA the governor sheds the shell hard, so on a big hit with loud ears the shell can go almost black, and full-shell white comes out gray. So on hits: dark ears on the first beat if the shell has the picture, or give the moment to the ears on purpose. `python3 $KIT/scenepower.py "<base>"` prints per scene the mA, how often the governor hit, shell and ear means, and heat.
