# Overnight, 29–30 October: what changed and what to do this morning

## Before you wear it

1. **Reload `mau5.htm` on your phone.** The new page is already on the
   controller (last tested on the helmet at 05:08). A tab left open since
   last night is still running last night's code.
2. **Set your phone's auto-lock to the longest setting** and don't press the
   power button during a set. The helmet's page is plain http, which browsers
   don't give the screen wake lock. The page now plays a tiny muted video to
   keep the screen on instead, but that trick hasn't been tried on your phone.
   The new `screen` stat on the page says which method is holding it.
3. **Look at the new `lag` stat** next to fps. It's how long a frame takes to
   reach the LEDs. If it reads 100 ms or more, your phone's WiFi is what's
   limiting the frame rate (see below).
4. Stopping the page now switches the helmet **off**. Before, every stop left
   it at solid orange, full brightness, because the controller has no standby
   preset saved. If you want the glow-when-idle back, run
   `python3 mau5offline.py standby --host 192.168.10.172 --fx pacifica --bri 96 --apply`.

## The jitter, honestly

You were right that it wasn't fixed. There turned out to be five separate
causes, and the last one can't be fixed on this firmware:

| Cause | What it looked like | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The power guard rescaled the whole shell every frame whenever the ears changed (up to 14×), and hard-clipped it at 64 | Shell pumping; eyes turning into flat discs whose edges popped | **Fixed.** Cuts still happen instantly, light comes back gradually, and a soft knee replaces the clip. Shell jitter on eq is down 35–60%. |
| Live audio bands took the log of a value already in decibels, had no smoothing, and eq bars were on/off per LED | eq twitching | **Fixed.** Correct bands, a meter-style fast rise and slow fall, and soft bar tops. |
| The show beat pulse was shorter than two phone frames | Random-height flicker on every cue | **Fixed.** A 70 ms hold, then decay. |
| The phone waited for each frame to be acknowledged before sending the next | Frame rate = 1 / WiFi round trip. A phone that dozes between packets gets 5–8 fps, which is "chains going slowly" | **Can't fix on WLED 0.15.4** (next row) |
| WLED 0.15.4 can't parse JSON pixels fast enough, and **restarts** when pushed harder | | See below |

On the laptop, every preset now runs at a median of **28 fps** (it was 6, with
a freeze after 90 s). On a phone with a slow round trip it will be lower.

### Why the controller limits it

I tried keeping two or three frames in flight. On a simulated phone that
roughly tripled the frame rate. But in every monitored test it **rebooted
the controller**: free memory slid from 118 kB to under 80 kB, then it
restarted. With one frame in flight it ran sweep after sweep with no
restart, so that's what's deployed.

Restarts can still happen under long heavy load, so the page now **recovers
on its own**. Tested with a real restart mid-show: the helmet was dark for
about 8 seconds, then came back at 26 fps with no tap. It also recovered
from an unplanned restart in the middle of a test sweep.

What seems to cause the restarts: WLED 0.15.4 sends a small reply for every
pixel message, about 100 a second, to whichever device sent it, and that
slowly breaks up its memory. Every crash tonight happened with **two or
more devices connected** to the controller. With a deliberately silent
extra connection, it crashed in about 70 seconds; with only the page
connected, it ran for 7 minutes without one. So at a show:

- **Have the page open in one tab on one phone.** Close old tabs; reload
  instead of opening a new one.
- **Don't have the WLED app, or WLED's own web page, open at the same time.**

### The real fix: new controller firmware

WLED 16.0.1 (stable since July) lets the phone send frames in a compact binary
form: three times smaller, with no JSON parsing on the controller and no
reply for each message. That should remove both the speed limit and the
memory pressure behind the restarts. It's a firmware flash,
so it's your call, and it's not something to do the morning of a show. When
you want it, I'll update the page to use it and test it first.

Until then, the smoothest option is still the laptop (`mau5ctl`/`mau5show`
send frames straight to the controller at 40 fps), with the phone as the
remote.

## Everything else that was fixed

A 195-agent review found **60 verified problems**. Fixes for all of them are
merged, and the test suite went from 42 tests to **101, all passing**. The ones
that mattered most:

- **Critical (power safety):** one bad frame, for example from dragging a
  period slider to zero, permanently switched off the phone's power limiter
  and heat bucket until the page was reloaded. Fixed in both the phone and
  laptop guards, and sliders can no longer reach values that break the maths.
- **Render loop could die silently** in cycle mode, freezing the helmet on
  its last frame. It now skips the frame, or lets go cleanly.
- **Spotify follow undid stop** within a second, and relit the helmet while
  the page was hidden. Fixed. Polls no longer overlap, and a paused track no
  longer loops a one-second chunk.
- **The dead man** (the helmet going dark when the phone stops talking) now
  arms in the same request as the start, and if it has fired while the page
  is still running, the page turns the helmet back on.
- **Every request has a timeout**, so a controller that stops answering can
  no longer hang the page.
- Laptop tools: `mau5doctor` no longer recommends a higher current limit, and
  no longer reports a missing power profile as fine. The WLED limiter setting
  in `mau5offline config` now follows the sustained power budget. That change
  is code only; it isn't applied to the controller.
- Calibration: each pose file records which chain it was measured on.
  `mau5locate` measures the phone camera's lag (1.1–1.5 s) and waits it out.
  The hand walk is no longer overwritten.
- Security: favourite names in the laptop control page were inserted as raw
  HTML (stored XSS). Fixed.

## The LED you cut off (from last night)

That piece was one pixel: one driver chip under ten LEDs. The map is now 621,
remeasured from the side camera, and verified there.

## Spotify: what could be better

What's fixed is listed above. Ideas, roughly from biggest win to smallest:

1. **Take the laptop out of Spotify setup.** Today the first sign-in has to go
   through `mau5spotify.py` on the laptop (Spotify only accepts https or
   loopback addresses for sign-in), and then a handoff line is pasted into the
   phone. A small https page on vortex1.dev could do the sign-in on the phone
   itself and pass the token to `http://4.3.2.1/mau5.htm` in the URL fragment,
   which never leaves the phone. No laptop, no copy and paste. It needs a new
   page on your public site, so I haven't made it.
2. **A QR code for the handoff**, if you keep the laptop step:
   `mau5spotify.py handoff` would print one to scan.
3. **Say plainly when follow can't work.** On the helmet's own WiFi the phone
   has no internet unless mobile data stays on, so following Spotify silently
   stops. The page should show that.
4. **Prefer the phone's own copy of the song.** Phones already get the show
   songs from the laptop and keep them, which needs no internet at all. That
   should be the default at a show, with Spotify only for following at home.

## Custom shows: Strobe and Ghosts n Stuff are choreographed

Until now a show was a cue list: one stock preset per phrase, so every song
was the same handful of effects in a different order. Three designs were
drafted and judged overnight. The winner is now built, merged and on the
controller.

Each song now has a hand-written **treatment** in `shows/scenes/`:

- written in bars against the song's own bar map, with its own palettes and
  motifs that come back with the music;
- builds that grow, and a dark beat before each drop;
- drops on the bar the bass actually lands on: Strobe 217, Ghosts 32, 63 and
  160 (the analysis corrected the first draft's guesses);
- the song title across the face once;
- call and response between the left half with ear 1 and the right half with
  ear 2;
- the ears as their own instrument.

How it was checked:

- The phone and the laptop render it identically, to 0.0005 counts out of
  255.
- It costs about 0.03 ms per frame.
- Every scene was run through the power guard: Strobe peaks at 39% of the
  heat bucket and Ghosts at 21%, and no limit was changed.
- On the helmet, Strobe played for a minute at 22–28 fps with no errors.

To play them, pick the show as before. On the laptop:
`python3 mau5show.py play "shows/deadmau5 - Strobe.json"`. To get the old cue
list back, add `?cues` to the page address on the phone, or set `MAU5_CUES=1`
on the laptop. The other three shows are unchanged.

**Worth a look first:** Strobe from 6:41 (the build at bar 213 into the drop
at 217) and Ghosts from 1:45 (bar 61 into the drop at 63). Watch from the
side as well as the front, and feel how warm the helmet is after a full song.
The treatments are JSON and meant to be edited; `python3 mau5scene.py` checks
and compiles them.

## Decisions for you

- Flash WLED 16.0.1 (the real fix for frame rate). Not before a show.
- Save a standby preset so the helmet glows when the page stops (command
  above).
- Lower the shell's `shed` in `data/cal/power.json` from 6 to 3–4: less
  pumping when the governor works. It's a power-config choice, so it's yours.
- Apply the new conservative WLED limiter (`mau5offline.py config --apply`).
  It only affects WLED's own effects, such as standby.
- Optionally set WLED's LED count to 621 (it drives 622; the last one is dark).
